MINI-PAPER: Odor + shock gives a T-maze memory (Tully & Quinn 1985)
  Tully T, Quinn WG (1985) Classical conditioning and retention in normal and mutant Drosophila melanogaster. Journal of Comparative Physiology A 157:263-277
  doi:10.1007/BF01350033   read: abstract only (Europe PMC record of PubMed 3939242); the full text was not read

  The paper reports: Twelve shock pulses in the presence of the first odor (and none with the second) were given and flies were tested in a T-maze. 95% of trained flies avoided the shock-associated odor. Learning reached a plateau within one training cycle and was resistant to extinction. Backward conditioning produced no learning, and nonassociative controls slightly reduced avoidance equally for both odors without affecting the associative learning index. Memory in wild-type flies decayed gradually over the first seven hours and was still present 24 h later.
